Access to Health Services Across Australian States and Territories

Healthcare access varies significantly across Australia due to differences in population size, geography and infrastructure. This visualisation explores how hospitals, pharmacies and community services are distributed across states and territories using both raw counts and population-adjusted measures.

```

Executive Summary

Healthcare infrastructure across Australia broadly follows population size, however population-adjusted measures reveal important differences in service accessibility. While larger states contain more healthcare facilities overall, smaller states and territories can provide higher levels of service per resident. This report explores these patterns through a series of visualisations comparing population, healthcare infrastructure, service composition and accessibility.

```

1. Where Do Australians Live?

Understanding population distribution provides important context for healthcare demand across Australia. Larger populations typically require more healthcare services and infrastructure, although facility availability does not always increase proportionally with population size.

Population

Population by State and Territory

Australia's population is concentrated within a small number of states, creating different levels of demand for healthcare infrastructure and services across the country.

Key Insight: New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland account for the majority of Australia's population, creating substantially greater healthcare demand than smaller states and territories.
Infrastructure

Total Health Facilities by State

This chart compares the total number of healthcare facilities available in each state and territory, providing an overview of healthcare infrastructure distribution nationwide.

Key Insight: States with larger populations generally contain more healthcare facilities, suggesting that infrastructure is concentrated where demand is highest.
Relationship

Population Versus Healthcare Infrastructure

Comparing population size against healthcare facility counts helps identify whether infrastructure grows proportionally with demand. Bubble size provides an additional indication of healthcare capacity.

Key Insight: A strong positive relationship exists between population size and healthcare infrastructure, although some states appear to provide relatively more facilities than would be expected based solely on population.

2. What Healthcare Services Are Available?

Healthcare systems rely on a mixture of hospitals, pharmacies and community services. The following visualisations examine how these facility types are distributed throughout Australia and how service composition varies between states and territories.

Service Mix

Health Facility Types by State

Different states rely on varying combinations of hospitals, pharmacies and community services. This chart highlights how healthcare service provision differs across Australia.

Key Insight: The composition of healthcare facilities differs across states, indicating that healthcare delivery relies on varying combinations of hospitals, pharmacies and community services.
Composition

Facility Composition by State

This stacked comparison provides a consolidated view of healthcare infrastructure, making it easier to compare the balance of facility types between states and territories.

Key Insight: While pharmacies often form the largest component of healthcare infrastructure, the balance between service types varies noticeably between states and territories.
Distribution

National Distribution of Facility Types

This chart summarises Australia's healthcare network by showing the overall contribution of each facility category to the national healthcare system.

Key Insight: Community-based services and pharmacies collectively make up a substantial proportion of Australia's healthcare network, highlighting the importance of primary healthcare accessibility.

3. Which States Have Better Access?

While larger states naturally contain more healthcare facilities, accessibility is better assessed using population-adjusted measures. The visualisations below compare healthcare availability across Australia using per-capita indicators and geographic representations.

Geography

Pharmacies per 100,000 Residents Across Australia

This choropleth map visualises pharmacy availability relative to population size. Darker states indicate a higher number of pharmacies per 100,000 residents, helping reveal geographic differences in access to essential healthcare services and medications.

Key Insight: Population-adjusted measures reveal accessibility differences that may not be visible when examining raw facility counts alone.
Accessibility

Hospitals per 100,000 Residents by State

The grid map simplifies Australia's geography into equally sized tiles, allowing hospital availability to be compared directly between states and territories. Colour intensity represents the number of hospitals per 100,000 residents.

Key Insight: Some smaller states and territories achieve higher hospital availability per resident despite having fewer hospitals overall.
Density

Health Service Density Heatmap

This heatmap compares the density of hospitals, pharmacies and community services across states and territories. Colour intensity highlights where healthcare services are relatively more abundant on a per-capita basis.

Key Insight: Different facility types exhibit different accessibility patterns across Australia.
Accessibility

Healthcare Services per 100,000 Residents

This dot plot ranks states according to the number of healthcare services available per 100,000 residents. By adjusting for population size, the chart provides a fair comparison of healthcare accessibility.

Key Insight: States with fewer total facilities may still provide strong accessibility when measured on a per-capita basis.
Comparison

Healthcare Service Profiles by State

The radar chart compares hospitals, pharmacies and community services simultaneously, providing a multi-dimensional view of healthcare infrastructure across Australian states and territories.

Key Insight: Each state exhibits a distinct healthcare profile, reflecting different approaches to healthcare service provision.
```

Key Findings

  • Population size strongly influences the number of healthcare facilities available.
  • Pharmacies represent one of the largest categories of healthcare infrastructure nationwide.
  • Population-adjusted measures reveal meaningful differences in healthcare accessibility.
  • Smaller states and territories can achieve higher service density than larger states.
  • Healthcare accessibility depends on both infrastructure quantity and geographic distribution.
```
